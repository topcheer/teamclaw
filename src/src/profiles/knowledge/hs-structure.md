# HS Classification Reference Pack

> As of: 2026-11-08. This pack carries the stable structure of the
> Harmonized System and the classification-proposal discipline only. It
> deliberately contains NO duty or rebate rate figures. Verify against
> authoritative sources before relying on any detail (the WCO HS nomenclature,
> the destination country's current tariff schedule, and customs publications);
> tariff schedules and national subdivisions are amended frequently.

## What the Harmonized System is

- The Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System ("HS") is the
  international commodity nomenclature maintained by the World Customs
  Organization (WCO) and used by customs administrations worldwide as the
  basis for customs tariffs, export/import statistics, and trade controls.
- The WCO reviews the nomenclature on a multi-year cycle (recent editions:
  HS 2017, HS 2022; a further review cycle is in preparation). A code
  memorized under an older edition may have been renumbered, split, or
  deleted - which is why codes are always proposed, never asserted.

## Structure: why "the first six digits" is the common language

- Section level (roman numerals) -> chapter (2 digits) -> heading
  (4 digits) -> subheading (6 digits). Example shape: 2-digit chapter,
  4-digit heading, 6-digit subheading - each level narrows the goods
  description.
- The first SIX digits are internationally harmonized: the same 6-digit
  code denotes the same goods category in every HS contracting party.
- Everything beyond digit six is NATIONAL: each administration extends the
  nomenclature for its own tariff and statistics, so the same goods carry
  different 8+ digit codes in different countries. Common extension shapes:
  8-digit domestic tariff lines with 10-digit inspection/CIQ suffixes
  (China), 8-digit Combined Nomenclature feeding 10-digit TARIC (EU),
  10-digit HTSUS statistical suffixes (US). Treat only the first six digits
  as comparable across borders.
- Chapters 98 and 99 and similar special chapters are reserved for national
  special-purpose classifications and are not part of the harmonized core.

## Classification logic (the General Rules for Interpretation, "GRI")

- GRI 1: classification is determined first by the words of the headings and
  section/chapter notes - notes override plausible readings.
- GRI 2: incomplete or unfinished articles with the essential character of
  the complete article, and goods presented unassembled/disassembled, are
  classified as the complete article; mixtures and composites are handled
  per GRI 2(b)/3.
- GRI 3: when two or more headings seem to apply - (a) the most specific
  description wins over more general ones; (b) otherwise classify by the
  material or component giving the goods their ESSENTIAL CHARACTER; (c)
  otherwise the heading occurring LAST in numerical order.
- GRI 4: goods not covered by any heading are classified under the heading
  of the goods to which they are MOST AKIN.
- GRI 5: cases and packing materials are classified with the goods,
  subject to stated exceptions (containers clearly suitable for repeated
  use, or packaging that gives the whole its essential character).
- GRI 6: subheading comparison works like heading comparison, but only
  between subheadings AT THE SAME LEVEL, applying section/chapter notes
  mutatis mutandis.

## Proposal discipline (how this team uses HS codes)

- Every code this team produces is a PROPOSAL with stated reasoning:
  product function, material/composition, and the GRI rule applied at each
  step where alternatives existed.
- The binding classification rests with customs authorities and the licensed
  broker - never present a code as definitive, and never present a national
  8+ digit extension as if it were internationally comparable.
- Where commercial stakes justify it, recommend the jurisdiction's advance
  ruling / pre-classification mechanism (for example the EU Binding Tariff
  Information, US customs ruling practice, or China customs
  pre-classification/pre-ruling channels) - the mechanism names are stable,
  their procedures and validity periods are not; check the current rules.
- A proposal memo names: candidate 6-digit subheading(s) with reasoning,
  the open national-extension question for the destination country, the
  documents that will settle it (composition sheets, lab reports, technical
  drawings), and who holds the binding decision.

## Export rebate (VAT rebate) discipline - no figures by design

- Export VAT/consumption-tax rebate treatment is keyed to commodity codes
  and is set and adjusted by the competent finance and taxation authorities
  in published batches that change over time.
- This pack therefore carries ZERO rate figures: rebate rates, catalog
  membership, and documentation windows are looked up in the CURRENT
  authoritative publication at the time of use (tax and customs portals the
  user supplies or confirms) and are marked TBD until then.
- Never quote a rebate rate from memory, never estimate one from a
  "similar" product, and never let a customer decision rest on an unsourced
  rate: deliver the document-set checklist and the lookup path instead.

## Standing discipline for this pack

- Prefer these structural definitions over recollection when explaining
  HS logic, and keep proposals in the proposal voice (candidate + reasoning
  + who decides).
- Anything rate-, duty-, or deadline-shaped is time-sensitive: direct the
  user to current authoritative sources and mark TBD.
