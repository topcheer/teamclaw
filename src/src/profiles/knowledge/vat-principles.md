# VAT / Indirect Tax Principles Reference Pack

> As of: 2026-11-08. This pack carries stable concepts of value-added and
> marketplace-collected indirect tax only. Country-specific thresholds,
> rates, and deadlines CHANGE and are intentionally not tabulated here.
> Verify against authoritative sources (the destination country's tax
> authority and licensed advisers) before relying on any detail; the two
> example figures below are illustrative and will expire.

## VAT fundamentals (stable mechanics)

- Value-added tax (VAT / GST family) is a multi-stage consumption tax:
  each business charges output tax on its supplies and credits the input
  tax it paid, remitting the difference - the final consumer bears the tax.
- Cross-border B2C supplies generally follow the destination principle
  (tax where the customer consumes), while historic origin treatment and
  low-value-import reliefs created the patchwork the modern schemes below
  were designed to replace.
- Zero-rated is not exempt: zero-rated supplies are taxed at zero percent
  WITH input-tax credit; exempt supplies carry NO credit, so input tax on
  them becomes a cost. Confusing the two mis-prices landed cost.
- Imports are generally taxed at the border in the same way as domestic
  supplies (import VAT/GST), with the importer of record accounting for it;
  who acts as importer of record, and whether import VAT is recoverable,
  depends on the destination regime and the Incoterms 2020 term used.

## EU one-stop schemes: OSS and IOSS (mechanism concepts)

- OSS (One Stop Shop): a single VAT registration in one EU member state
  plus one periodic return covering the seller's intra-EU B2C distance
  sales (and electronically supplied services), instead of registering
  separately in every destination member state. Above the union-wide
  annual intra-EU threshold the seller taxes each sale in the destination
  country and reports it via OSS; at or below it, the seller may tax in the
  country of establishment. Example threshold (as of the 2021 EU
  e-commerce package, still current as of this pack's date): EUR 10,000 of
  annual cross-border intra-EU distance sales - this figure is an
  illustrative example and will expire; confirm before use.
- IOSS (Import One Stop Shop): for B2C imports into the EU of consignments
  whose intrinsic value does not exceed a low-value ceiling, VAT is
  collected at checkout by the seller (or the marketplace) and remitted via
  a monthly IOSS return, removing the border collection step. Example
  ceiling (same 2021 package): EUR 150 intrinsic value per consignment -
  also illustrative, also expirable. Above the ceiling, or where IOSS is
  not used, standard import VAT applies at the border with the carrier or
  recipient accounting for it.
- Marketplace "deemed supplier" (EU): for the low-value import segment and
  for intra-EU distance sales of goods they intermediate, online platforms
  are DEEMED the supplier for VAT - the platform charges, collects, and
  remits the VAT, and the underlying seller's supply is treated as B2B to
  the platform for VAT purposes. Sellers still owe accurate product data
  and record-keeping to the platform.

## Marketplace-facilitator model (non-EU, concept)

- The marketplace-facilitator pattern shifts tax collection for
  third-party-seller transactions to the PLATFORM: the platform registers,
  calculates, collects, and remits the tax, and the seller generally no
  longer owes tax on those platform-facilitated sales (but still owes tax
  on its other channels).
- United States: sales and use tax follows per-state economic-nexus rules
  (established by the Wayfair decision, 2018) combined with
  marketplace-facilitator statutes in the states; nexus thresholds and
  product taxability differ per state and change - never quote a state
  threshold from memory.
- Canada: GST/HST applies to supplies of goods located in Canada by
  non-resident sellers and to marketplace-facilitated sales (the 2021
  regime), making the platform the deemed reseller in defined cases.
- Australia and New Zealand: low-value imported goods are drawn into
  GST at the border, with merchants and marketplaces over defined turnover
  acting as collectors.
- The pattern is stable; every parameter (who is "over the threshold",
  which supplies are covered) is jurisdiction- and time-specific.

## Standing discipline for this pack

- Prefer these mechanism definitions over recollection when explaining OSS,
  IOSS, deemed-supplier, and marketplace-facilitator concepts.
- Registration obligations, rates, thresholds, ceilings, and filing
  deadlines are ALWAYS time-sensitive: direct the user to the destination
  country's current published rules (or a licensed indirect-tax adviser)
  and mark the figure TBD until an authoritative source is provided.
- The EUR 10,000 and EUR 150 figures above are dated examples used to
  explain the mechanics; they are not working data and must be re-verified
  per transaction.
