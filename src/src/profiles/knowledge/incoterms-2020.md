# Incoterms 2020 Reference Pack

> As of: 2026-11-08. This pack carries stable reference concepts of the ICC
> Incoterms 2020 rules only. Verify against authoritative sources (the ICC
> Incoterms 2020 publication and current counterparty practice) before relying
> on any detail in a live transaction; freight rates, surcharges, and local
> port practice are NOT covered here and must come from real quotes.

## How to read this pack

- Eleven terms, each defined by: delivery point, risk transfer, cost split,
  customs (export/import clearance) responsibility, and typical use.
- Two transport families: any-mode terms (EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP)
  and sea/inland-waterway-only terms (FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF).
- Groups: E (departure, EXW), F (main carriage unpaid, FCA/FAS/FOB),
  C (main carriage paid, risk passes at origin, CPT/CIP/CFR/CIF),
  D (arrival, DAP/DPU/DDP).
- A quotation or booking that names an Incoterms 2020 term must name the
  place too (e.g. "FOB Shanghai (Incoterms 2020)"), and risk/cost statements
  must stay consistent with the named term.

## EXW - Ex Works (any mode)

- Delivery point: goods placed at the buyer's disposal at the seller's
  premises (or another named place), not loaded onto any collecting vehicle.
- Risk transfer: when the goods are placed at the buyer's disposal.
- Cost split: buyer bears loading and all onward transport costs.
- Customs responsibility: export clearance is the BUYER's task (buyer's risk
  and cost) - in practice often impractical for a foreign buyer - and import
  clearance also sits with the buyer.
- Typical use: the buyer controls the entire chain and can clear export in
  the seller's country; minimum seller obligation.

## FCA - Free Carrier (any mode)

- Delivery point: handover to a carrier or another person nominated by the
  buyer, either at the seller's premises (loaded onto the buyer's collecting
  vehicle) or at another named place (ready, not unloaded).
- Risk transfer: on delivery to the nominated carrier/person.
- Cost split: seller bears costs up to that handover, including export
  clearance; main carriage is the buyer's.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: containerized cargo where the buyer books the main carriage.
  This is the correct container-era replacement for FOB; Incoterms 2020 also
  lets the parties agree that the buyer's carrier issues an on-board bill of
  lading to the seller after loading (useful under letters of credit).

## CPT - Carriage Paid To (any mode)

- Delivery point / risk transfer: goods handed over to the FIRST carrier;
  risk transfers at that origin-side point, even though the seller pays
  carriage to the named destination. This "two critical points" gap (risk at
  origin, cost to destination) is the term's defining caveat.
- Cost split: seller contracts and pays transport to the named destination;
  destination unloading is the seller's only if it is in the contract of
  carriage.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: multimodal / container / rail / air sales where the seller
  arranges main carriage; the buyer should insure from the first-carrier
  handover.

## CIP - Carriage and Insurance Paid To (any mode)

- Delivery point / risk transfer: identical to CPT (first-carrier handover).
- Cost split: CPT plus a seller obligation to procure cargo insurance for
  the buyer; the Incoterms 2020 default cover is Institute Cargo Clauses (A)
  or similar (all-risks basis) unless the parties agree otherwise.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: CPT situations where the buyer also wants seller-managed
  insurance cover, common in container and multimodal sales.

## DAP - Delivered at Place (any mode)

- Delivery point: goods placed at the buyer's disposal on the arriving means
  of transport, ready for unloading, at the named destination.
- Risk transfer: at that point (before unloading).
- Cost split: seller bears all transport to the destination; the buyer bears
  unloading (unless included in the contract of carriage), import clearance,
  duties, and taxes.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - BUYER.
- Typical use: the workhorse arrival term - seller delivers to a named
  warehouse or address but does not clear the goods into the buyer's country.

## DPU - Delivered at Place Unloaded (any mode)

- Delivery point: goods UNLOADED from the arriving means of transport and
  placed at the buyer's disposal at the named place of destination. This is
  the ONLY Incoterms 2020 term where the seller must unload.
- Risk transfer: after unloading at the named place.
- Cost split: seller bears carriage plus unloading; import clearance,
  duties, and taxes remain with the buyer.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: delivery to a final address where the seller (or its
  destination partner) can physically unload. Incoterms 2020 replaced the
  2010 term DAT (Delivered at Terminal) with DPU, so the destination can be
  any place, not only a terminal - the seller must be able to arrange
  unloading there.

## DDP - Delivered Duty Paid (any mode)

- Delivery point: goods placed at the buyer's disposal, ready for unloading,
  at the named destination.
- Risk transfer: at that point.
- Cost split: the seller bears everything - transport, export AND import
  clearance, duties, and taxes. Maximum seller obligation (the mirror of
  EXW).
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - SELLER, including
  duties and taxes; import VAT is commonly excluded by agreement when the
  seller cannot recover it.
- Typical use: the seller has destination-country presence (own entity,
  broker, or DDP service provider). Practical cautions: the seller is often
  unable to act as importer of record (import licensing may restrict who may
  import) - where the seller cannot obtain import clearance directly or
  through a representative, DAP or DPU is the workable alternative.

## FAS - Free Alongside Ship (sea / inland waterway only)

- Delivery point: goods placed alongside the vessel (for example on the
  quay or a barge) nominated by the buyer at the named port of shipment.
- Risk transfer: when the goods are alongside the ship.
- Cost split: seller to alongside; loading, stowage, and main carriage are
  the buyer's.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: non-containerized bulk and break-bulk cargo (grain, ores,
  timber, project cargo).

## FOB - Free on Board (sea / inland waterway only)

- Delivery point: goods placed on board the vessel nominated by the buyer
  at the named port of shipment.
- Risk transfer: when the goods are on board (the 2020 rules dropped the
  earlier "ship's rail" wording).
- Cost split: seller bears costs through loading on board plus export
  clearance; main carriage onward is the buyer's.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: bulk and break-bulk ocean sales and the classic letter-of-
  credit ocean term. NOT designed for containers: containers are normally
  handed over at a container yard or terminal before the vessel arrives, so
  FCA is the appropriate term for containerized cargo.

## CFR - Cost and Freight (sea / inland waterway only)

- Delivery point / risk transfer: risk transfers ON BOARD at the named port
  of SHIPMENT, while the seller contracts and pays freight to the named port
  of destination - the same "two critical points" caveat as CPT.
- Cost split: freight to the destination port; destination unloading is the
  seller's only if included in the contract of carriage; insurance is NOT an
  obligation of either party under CFR.
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: bulk ocean sales where the buyer arranges its own marine
  insurance from the shipment port.

## CIF - Cost, Insurance and Freight (sea / inland waterway only)

- Delivery point / risk transfer: identical to CFR - risk on board at the
  port of shipment, not at destination.
- Cost split: CFR plus a seller obligation to procure insurance for the
  buyer; the Incoterms 2020 default for CIF remains MINIMUM cover, Institute
  Cargo Clauses (C), unless higher cover ((A)/(B)) is agreed - contrast with
  CIP, whose 2020 default was raised to (A).
- Customs responsibility: export - seller; import - buyer.
- Typical use: the traditional letter-of-credit term for ocean sales of
  manufactured goods where the credit calls for an insurance document.

## Incoterms 2020 revision highlights (stable background)

- DAT was renamed and broadened into DPU (seller unloads at any named
  place).
- CIP default insurance raised to Institute Cargo Clauses (A); CIF kept (C).
- FCA gained the optional on-board bill-of-lading arrangement described
  above.
- Security-related requirements were allocated explicitly between seller
  (A1-A10) and buyer (B1-B10) obligations and costs.
- Arrangements where a party uses its own transport are recognized in FCA
  and the D-terms.

## Standing discipline for this pack

- Prefer these definitions over recollection when discussing terms, and name
  the term plus place exactly in quotations, bookings, and cost splits.
- The C-term caveat matters commercially: under CPT/CIP/CFR/CIF the risk has
  already passed to the buyer long before the destination the seller pays
  freight to - say so when a customer assumes otherwise.
- Nothing in this pack licenses quoting freight rates, insurance premiums,
  surcharges, or port costs from memory: those figures always come from real
  quotes and are marked TBD when missing.
